I joined MeloMed because I wanted to experience an early-stage startup from the inside: how a product gets built from scratch, and how the business side fits around it. It caught my attention because it uniquely combines artificial intelligence with music, health, and wellness. MeloMed's product includes a journalling app that connects to Spotify. AI analyses your journal entries and listening patterns to understand your music preferences, then generates music for you.

As the product manager, I worked on user research and interviews, UI and UX fixes, and Instagram marketing. It was also a chance to put what I'd learnt in USC's TAC 466 (Building a High-Tech Startup) into practice.

I've learnt that I can't judge my own product. When you build and design something, you naturally grow to like it, and that makes it easy to miss problems that are obvious to anyone seeing it for the first time. User interviews showed me how important outside feedback is, and how important it is to pick the right user segment to find product-market fit.

Communication matters most when you're remote. At one point, our team was spread across North America, Europe and Asia, so it was challenging to find a time to meet. I learnt to keep updates short, and to keep everyone's tasks and responsibilities organised and up to date, so work kept moving even when we couldn't talk.
